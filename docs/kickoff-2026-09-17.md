## Design directions for selection

### 1. Precision in steel — recommended

Subject: one authentic instrument cutout and prominent model search. Light: controlled highlights against a graphite hero, transitioning to pale technical catalogue surfaces. Framing: large off-centre product, left-aligned English headline, mirrored Arabic composition, generous space around search and quotation actions. Emotion: engineering confidence and dependable local support.

Use an editorial display face sparingly on marketing headings, a clear sans for technical data, and a compatible Arabic family. One brand-derived accent remains pending the supplied identity. Product pages prioritise the exact model, specifications, reviewed applications, availability label and quote action. A short hero depth/compression sequence provides the signature motion; reduced-motion and constrained devices use a static composition.

### 2. Technical reference

Subject: model search and product identification. Light: primarily pale neutral surfaces, charcoal text and metal product imagery. Framing: compact navigation, wide readable specification tables and category-led composition. Emotion: clarity, speed and precision. Best for repeat procurement and dense browsing; less cinematic than direction 1.

### 3. Factory expertise

Subject: genuine factory/service photography paired with instruments. Light: dark editorial photography with restrained warm highlights and neutral catalogue pages. Framing: asymmetric photography, short service narratives and clear category entry points. Emotion: human expertise and established operational trust. Depends on usable real factory photography and approved service claims.

All directions use mobile-first layouts, visible focus, sensible touch targets, RTL/LTR isolation for model codes and units, accessible forms, and usable content without animation. Avoid mandatory intros and scroll hijacking. Branding, images and final type choices await source assets. Nour selects the direction before UI implementation.

## Architecture baseline

One TypeScript Next.js application, Payload administration and one authoritative PostgreSQL database. Keep catalogue, RFQ and inventory logic in separate server modules. Use Payload authentication rather than a second staff identity system. Resolve compatible pinned versions using official installed-version documentation before scaffolding.

Public product content is server-rendered. Client components cover the basket and limited interactions. Cache approved catalogue content independently from stock. Public availability reveals labels, not internal quantities. Allocation always checks database rows in a transaction. Store UTC timestamps and display staff deadlines in Africa/Cairo.

Use public storage for approved catalogue assets and separate private quarantine for RFQ attachments. Background work covers notification outbox, reservation expiry, alerts and reconciliation. Hosting, database provider, storage, email, malware scanning and scheduler are choices to resolve before deploying; no services have been provisioned.

## Critical business behaviour

- Model records describe products; SKU records identify supplied configurations. Never generate combinations or infer certification/stock from catalogue presence.
- Unverified enquiries and model-only requests record demand without reserving inventory.
- Proposed policy: a single-use, hashed email token valid for 30 minutes; eligible verified SKU requests receive a 24-hour hold. This policy needs the stock owner's confirmation before go-live. Support a manual allocation trigger if selected explicitly.
- Available = on hand minus active reserved minus blocked. On-hand stock falls only on dispatch. Partial allocations record both held and unmet units.
- Lock affected balance rows in sorted SKU order. Reconcile expired holds under those locks. Save state transitions, ledger events and notification outbox atomically, then invalidate caches and send notifications after commit.
- Idempotency covers submission, confirmation, stock operations, imports and jobs. Repeating a key with changed input is rejected. Duplicate retries must not move stock twice.
- Partial dispatch preserves remaining allocation; decline/cancel/expiry releases the unconsumed remainder. Adjustments cannot reduce stock below protected units.
- Proposed stock freshness is 48 hours; stale stock displays Check availability and requires staff confirmation. Exact public quantities remain private.
- Keep RFQ, verification and reservation states independent. An accepted quotation does not bypass stock checks.
- Email delivery failure must retain the RFQ and expose retry status to staff.
- Owner, catalogue editor, sales and warehouse permissions apply to direct API requests as well as screens. Posted ledger entries are immutable.
- Proposed attachment policy: three files, ten MB each, PDF/JPEG/PNG/XLSX, validated and scanned before authorised short-lived downloads.

## Implementation increments

1. Foundation: compatible pinned stack, environment template, isolated development database, CMS roles, bilingual catalogue schemas and draft gates. Validate product/SKU import data before writes. Keep synthetic fixtures visibly separate from publishable content.
2. Catalogue slice: selected homepage and one category/product template in both languages, ten client-reviewed products, search/filter behaviour and local quote basket. Review desktop/mobile EN/AR appearance.
3. RFQ slice: atomic submission and immutable snapshots, safe verification, private upload flow and retrying outbox. Prove saved requests survive email failure.
4. Stock slice: one real identified SKU in isolated test data, ledger, reservation lifecycle and concurrency tests using PostgreSQL. Verify the complete catalogue-to-RFQ-to-reservation flow before scaling content.
5. Completion: controlled catalogue batches, staff operations, four industries, about/contact, SEO, analytics and the agreed motion sequence. Add no unapproved claims or fabricated commercial data.
6. Release candidate: run T01–T14, reconciliation, migration/restore/rollback rehearsals and staff training. Present evidence and outstanding decisions for release authorization.

Quality targets: lab performance reports for representative devices, LCP <=2.5s, INP <=200ms and CLS <=0.1 as field targets. A lab score is not proof of field compliance. Reduced-motion, no-JavaScript reading, keyboard completion, private-data boundaries and database-backed race tests are required.
